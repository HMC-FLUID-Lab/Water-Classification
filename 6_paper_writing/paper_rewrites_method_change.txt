EDIT 1  —  paragraph at Draft5.tex line 118  (motivation: valley vs tails)
================================================================================
FIND: "The failures of K-means and GMM indicate that there is a substantial population ... we choose the density-based spatial clustering of applications with noise (DBSCAN) for this purpose. \cite{ester_density-based_1996}"

REPLACE WITH:
The failures of K-means and GMM indicate a substantial population of molecules in the
transition region between the two states, which must be set aside before the remaining
molecules can be classified cleanly. If water is genuinely a two-state mixture, these
transition molecules are configurations interconverting between LFTS and DNLS, and they
occupy the low-density valley between the two modes of the
distribution~\cite{russo_understanding_2014,shi_direct_2020}; were water a single
continuum, no such valley would exist and the strategy would fail. A density-based
analysis is therefore the natural tool. Low feature-point density, however, arises in two
physically distinct places: the central valley \emph{between} the states, and the outer
\emph{tails} of each state, where molecules are confidently one structure but at extreme
order-parameter values. A method that discards all low-density points---for instance, by
taking the noise label of DBSCAN~\cite{ester_density-based_1996} directly---would remove
genuine LFTS and DNLS molecules from the tails along with the ambiguous ones. We therefore
build a density-based procedure that cleaves only the middle.

EDIT 2  —  paragraphs at Draft5.tex lines 121 AND 123  (the three-step mechanism)
================================================================================
FIND: the two paragraphs beginning "DBSCAN groups feature points that lie in contiguous
high-density regions ..." (L121) and "Thus, our strategy for identifying the two states
is developed as follows ..." (L123).

REPLACE BOTH WITH (two paragraphs):

Our procedure has three steps. First, we apply DBSCAN~\cite{ester_density-based_1996} not
as a classifier but to locate the two dense cores of the distribution and to reject gross
outliers; the clean cores then seed a two-component Gaussian mixture, so the mixture locks
onto the two lobes rather than being distorted by sparse points. Second, the fitted mixture
assigns \emph{every} molecule---including those in the tails---to the state of higher
posterior probability~\cite{bishop_pattern_2006,dempster_maximum_1977}, so no confident
molecule is discarded. Third, we identify the transition set from the density minimum
between the two states: projecting the molecules onto Fisher's linear discriminant axis,
which optimally separates the two fitted components~\cite{fisher_use_1936}, we estimate the
one-dimensional density along this axis and label as transitional only the molecules lying
in the low-density valley between the modes, where the two states' memberships are
comparable~\cite{menard_fuzzy_2000}. This is a three-way decision---LFTS, DNLS, or
transition---in which the third outcome is reserved for genuinely ambiguous configurations
rather than for distant outliers~\cite{yao_three-way_2010}.

The width of the transition band is set by a single interpretable parameter: the density
threshold, expressed as a fraction of the two modal peak heights, below which a molecule in
the valley is deemed transitional. Because the tails remain assigned to their states, this
places the cleavage in the middle of the distribution---a clean cut through the sparse
crossover---rather than around the entire low-density periphery. The neighborhood radius
$\varepsilon$ and minimum count MinPts of the core-finding step, together with this valley
depth, are chosen by maximizing the silhouette score of the resulting classification
(Supplementary Note~2 and Supplementary Fig.~S5). The full pipeline is illustrated in
Fig.~\ref{fig:water_classification_workflow}.

EDIT 10  —  Methods, "Hybrid Density--GMM Pipelines" subsection, Draft5.tex lines 308-309
================================================================================
FIND: the "\subsubsection{Hybrid Density--GMM Pipelines}" body beginning
"DBSCAN~\cite{ester_density-based_1996} identifies clusters as contiguous high-density
regions ... are selected via a systematic grid search (Supplementary Note~2 and Supplementary
Figs.~S5 and~S6)."

REPLACE the subsection title and body WITH:

\subsubsection{Density-Valley GMM Pipeline}
Our main pipeline combines density-based core identification with probabilistic assignment
and an explicit three-way decision. We first run DBSCAN~\cite{ester_density-based_1996} on the
standardized features to locate the two dense cores and reject gross outliers; DBSCAN serves
only this purpose, not final classification, because its cluster count depends sensitively on
the neighborhood radius. A two-component Gaussian mixture is seeded on the DBSCAN cores and
fitted by expectation--maximization~\cite{dempster_maximum_1977,bishop_pattern_2006}, and every
molecule is assigned to the component of higher posterior probability, so molecules in the
distribution tails are retained in their states. To identify ambiguous molecules we project all
points onto Fisher's linear discriminant axis between the two fitted
components~\cite{fisher_use_1936}, estimate the probability density along this axis by kernel
density estimation, and locate the minimum between the two modes. Molecules that lie between
the modes with a density below a chosen fraction (the valley depth) of the modal peak heights
are labelled transitional~\cite{menard_fuzzy_2000,yao_three-way_2010}; all others keep their
mixture assignment. The DBSCAN parameters $\varepsilon$ and MinPts and the valley-depth fraction
are selected by a grid search that maximizes the silhouette score~\cite{rousseeuw_silhouettes_1987}
of the resulting classification (Supplementary Note~2 and Supplementary Figs.~S5 and~S6). As a
density-native cross-check we replace the DBSCAN-seeded core step with
HDBSCAN~\cite{mcinnes_hdbscan_2017,malzer_hybrid_2020}, which requires no explicit density radius.
